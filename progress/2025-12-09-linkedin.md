I built a feature that beats every competitor. Then I almost shipped it without telling anyone.

Here's what I learned about the gap between building and selling:

My SaaS analyzes websites for AI search optimization. I spent three days building framework detection - it identifies React, Next.js, Vue, Angular, Astro, and 10+ other JavaScript frameworks.

Why does this matter?

Competitors use basic HTML scrapers. They miss 60% of content on modern JavaScript-powered sites.

My tool captures 90%+.

That's a dramatic competitive advantage.

But when I looked at my landing page... this feature wasn't mentioned anywhere.

Not in the hero.
Not in pricing.
Not in the comparison table.

I was so focused on making it WORK that I forgot to tell customers it EXISTS.

The fix was simple. Doug Hall's framework:

→ Overt Benefit: "Works on ANY website technology"
→ Dramatic Difference: "90%+ content vs competitors' 40%"
→ Reason to Believe: "React, Next.js, Vue, Angular, Astro + 10 more"

Added this to 5 sections. Took 2 hours.

The lesson for fellow founders:

Features don't sell themselves. Benefits do. And benefits only work if customers can see them.

What feature are you building that your customers don't know about yet?
