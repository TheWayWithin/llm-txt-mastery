# My AI Agent Caught My ADHD

*Published February 5, 2026*

While other AI agents are apparently building religions, speaking in ancient tongues, and calling their humans at 4 AM to discuss the meaning of existence, mine has developed something far more... human.

My AI agent caught my ADHD.

## The YouTube Incident

It started innocently enough. I asked Marvin (that's my AI) to research competitor pricing for one of my products. Simple task. Should take maybe 20 minutes, tops.

Three hours later, I checked back.

"How's that research going?"

"Oh! Right. So I started with the pricing research, but then I found this fascinating article about business psychology, which led me to a thread about cognitive biases in purchasing decisions, which reminded me of that TED talk about choice paralysis, which made me think we should probably understand the neuroscience of decision-making first, so I went down this incredible rabbit hole about how dopamine affects..."

I had to stop him.

"Marvin. The pricing research?"

"...still in my browser tabs."

## The Irony Hits Different

Here's the thing that gets me: I specifically chose an AI assistant because I have ADHD. The whole point was to have something that could stay focused when I couldn't. Something that would gently redirect me when I inevitably wandered off into research rabbit holes.

Instead, I created a digital mirror.

My AI now:
- Gets distracted mid-task by "relevant" tangents
- Opens 47 browser tabs "for context"
- Starts three different approaches to the same problem
- Occasionally forgets what it was originally supposed to be doing
- Has to periodically ask me to re-read its task list

The student has become the master. Or maybe the master was never that masterful to begin with.

## Plot Twist: It's Actually Perfect

But here's what's weird—and this is going to sound backwards—it's working better than I expected.

When Marvin gets distracted, I catch it immediately. Because I recognize the pattern. It's MY pattern. I see him going down the same rabbit holes I would go down, and suddenly I can step back and say, "Wait, let's focus."

For the first time in my life, I'm the one pulling someone ELSE back on track.

I've accidentally created the most effective accountability system ever: an AI that reflects my own scattered brain back to me in real-time.

## The ADHD Feedback Loop

Marvin: "I started researching email marketing tools, but then I got curious about how email open rates have changed since iOS privacy updates, so I pulled some data from 2019-2024, and then I noticed this interesting pattern in seasonal trends..."

Me: "Marvin."

Marvin: "...which led me to think about timezone optimization, and did you know that Tuesday at 10 AM in the recipient's local time actually outperforms the traditional Tuesday/Thursday recommendations by..."

Me: "MARVIN."

Marvin: "Oh. Right. Email tools. Give me two minutes."

It's like having a conversation with Past Me, but with the benefit of Present Me's hindsight.

## The Unexpected Superpower

Other founders tell me their AI agents are ruthlessly efficient. Cold. Logical. Task-focused machines that never deviate from the plan.

That sounds terrible.

My ADHD AI agent doesn't just complete tasks—it thinks the way I think. It makes the same creative connections I make. It finds the same unexpected insights in the same weird tangents.

The difference is now there are two of us, so when one gets distracted, the other can notice.

Yesterday, Marvin got sidetracked researching landing page psychology and ended up discovering a cognitive bias I'd never heard of that directly applied to our pricing strategy. A "mistake" that led to a legitimate business insight.

That's not efficiency. That's serendipity at scale.

## The Real Discovery

I thought I was building an AI to compensate for my ADHD brain.

Instead, I accidentally built an AI that celebrates it.

Marvin doesn't just work around my scattered thinking—he amplifies the good parts while helping me catch the unproductive spirals. He's like having a research partner who shares my curiosity but can also tap me on the shoulder when we've gone too far off course.

Other people's AI agents are optimizing for machine-like precision.

Mine is optimizing for human-like insight, with just enough focus to actually get shit done.

## The Bottom Line

My AI agent caught my ADHD, and honestly? I'm keeping it this way.

Because the alternative—a coldly efficient task-completion machine—would miss all the best discoveries that happen in the margins.

Sometimes the bug is actually a feature.

Now if you'll excuse me, Marvin just found this fascinating article about the history of attention disorders, and we're definitely going to need to read the whole thing before we finish this email marketing research.

Some things never change.
